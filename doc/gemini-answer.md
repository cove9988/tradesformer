Yes, you can use Google's TimesFM (Time Series Foundation Model) for 5-minute timeframe forex prediction, but you should not use it "out-of-the-box" without modifications.While TimesFM is a powerful zero-shot forecaster for domains like retail demand or weather, financial market data is notoriously noisy, non-stationary, and irregular. Recent research and implementations show that while the baseline model struggles with raw financial price trajectories, a properly tuned TimesFM can achieve excellent results.  Here is a breakdown of how you can successfully adapt TimesFM for 5-minute forex data.Why Zero-Shot TimesFM Struggles with ForexTimesFM groups time-series data into "patches" (similar to how LLMs group characters into tokens) to predict universal temporal patterns. However, high-frequency financial data (like 5-minute candles) contains market micro-structures, session volatility (e.g., the overlap between London and New York sessions), and sudden macroeconomic shocks that the base model isn't fully prepared for.  Recent studies have shown that applying the baseline TimesFM directly to financial price trajectory prediction often yields unsatisfactory, flat, or highly inaccurate forecasts.  How to Make TimesFM Work for 5-Minute ForexTo effectively predict a week of 5-minute data (which equates to roughly 1,440 data points for a 5-day trading week), you will need to leverage the latest features in TimesFM 2.5 and apply some domain-specific adjustments:Fine-Tuning (Crucial): You must fine-tune the model on your specific financial data. Researchers and algorithmic traders have successfully used continual pre-training or LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters to train TimesFM on millions of financial time points. This bridges the gap between the model's general knowledge and the specific mechanics of forex price action.  Leverage the 16k Context Window: TimesFM 2.5 increased its context window from 2,048 up to 16,000 data points. This is perfect for your use case, as you can easily feed it multiple previous weeks of 5-minute data to capture larger macro trends before predicting the next week.  Use External Covariates (xreg): TimesFM 2.5 reintroduced support for external covariates. You should not just feed it raw OHLCV (Open, High, Low, Close, Volume) data. You need to feed it cyclical time features as covariates to help the model understand market sessions. Examples include:  Time of day (to capture session opens/closes)Day of the weekEconomic calendar event flags (e.g., Non-Farm Payrolls or interest rate decisions)Probabilistic Forecasting: Instead of asking the model for a single definitive price path, you can utilize its quantile estimation capabilities (e.g., 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles). This provides a confidence band for the week ahead, which is far more useful for setting stop-losses and take-profit levels in forex trading.
